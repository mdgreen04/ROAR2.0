## This week's top picks

1. **[Whole-pelvic RT does not improve survival over prostate-only RT in high-risk disease (5-trial IPD meta-analysis)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clon.2026.104329)**
   _Clin Oncol (R Coll Radiol) · Aug 22, 2026 · Genitourinary_ · meta-analysis · IPD · prostate · target volumes
   Five phase 3 trials (5,172 men) of elective whole-pelvic versus prostate-only RT were pooled with reconstructed individual-patient data. Overall survival was not improved (HR 1.07, 95% CI 0.94–1.21; reconstructed-IPD HR 0.98), and the apparent gains in biochemical/progression-free (HR 0.84) and metastasis-free survival (HR 0.92) disappeared once the single PSMA-staged, single-centre POP-RT trial was excluded (0.90 and 1.00). WPRT modestly increased late grade ≥2 GU and GI toxicity without a severe-event excess; certainty was moderate for OS and very low for the progression endpoints.
   **Why it matters:** Routine elective nodal RT remains unproven; the plausible beneficiaries are the very-high-nodal-risk, PSMA-staged patients POP-RT enrolled, and that subgroup needs prospective confirmation.
   [Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clon.2026.104329) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42727425/) · [U-M full text](https://proxy.lib.umich.edu/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clon.2026.104329)

2. **[OPEN phase 3: open face masks with surface guidance match closed-mask setup accuracy and patients prefer them](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111779)**
   _Radiother Oncol · Sep 12, 2026 · Head & neck_ · phase 3 · SGRT · immobilisation · patient experience
   230 patients receiving radical head-and-neck RT were randomised 1:1:1 to a standard closed 5-point mask without SGRT, an SGRT-supported open 3-point mask, or an open 5-point mask. Daily CBCT showed no difference in vertical, lateral or rotational setup error; the only significant difference was larger longitudinal shifts in the closed-mask arm (P < 0.001). Tolerability, overall experience and distress scores were significantly better with both open masks (p < 0.01).
   **Why it matters:** This is randomised support for switching to open-mask, SGRT-monitored immobilisation as a patient-centred standard without giving up accuracy.
   [Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111779) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42731759/) · [U-M full text](https://proxy.lib.umich.edu/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111779)

3. **[NEJM: B7-H3 ADC tambotatug pelitecan beats topotecan in relapsed SCLC — OS 13.3 vs 9.4 months, HR 0.46](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2610229)**
   _N Engl J Med · Sep 12, 2026 · Systemic therapy worth knowing_ · phase 3 · practice-changing · SCLC · ADC
   TAISHAN-302 randomised 451 patients with small-cell lung cancer progressing after first-line platinum to tambotatug pelitecan or topotecan. At the prespecified interim analysis, median OS was 13.3 vs 9.4 months (stratified HR for death 0.46, 95% CI 0.35–0.62), PFS 7.4 vs 2.8 months (HR 0.29) and confirmed response 59.1% vs 9.7%, with fewer grade ≥3 adverse events (55.4% vs 77.9%). Open-label, interim, industry-funded (MediLink).
   **Why it matters:** Second-line SCLC has had no real standard beyond topotecan for decades; expect this to change systemic sequencing and the population you see for thoracic and palliative RT.
   [Article](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2610229) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42734213/) · [U-M full text](https://proxy.lib.umich.edu/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmoa2610229)

4. **[PARTY: first prospective trial of online-adaptive proton SBRT — adaptation needed in 84% of fractions, 100% feasible](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.071)**
   _Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys · Sep 10, 2026 · Radiation oncology — practice, technique & physics_ · phase 1 · adaptive RT · protons · SBRT
   Ten patients with abdominal, pelvic or thoracic oligometastases received 30–50 CGE in 5 fractions with an in-room CT-on-rails online adaptive proton workflow. All 50 fractions were delivered adaptively (median door-to-door 88 minutes); 42 (84%) required adaptation, roughly half for OAR constraint violations and the rest for coverage, and adapted plans improved coverage and OAR dose even after accounting for intrafraction motion. One grade 3 toxicity of unclear attribution; 3- and 6-month local control 90% and 73% with 7.8 months' follow-up.
   **Why it matters:** Daily anatomy, not range uncertainty alone, is what has kept proton SBRT niche; this shows online adaptation is workable end to end.
   [Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.071) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42722207/) · [U-M full text](https://proxy.lib.umich.edu/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.071)

5. **[ctHPV DNA reappearance flags recurrence after RT for HPV+ oropharynx cancer with 98% negative predictive value](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108126)**
   _Oral Oncol · Sep 9, 2026 · Head & neck_ · biomarker · surveillance · HPV · oropharynx
   Serial quantitative-PCR ctHPV DNA surveillance in 232 baseline-positive patients (2,928 assessments) treated 2012–2024 at one institution. Among 220 complete responders, ctHPV DNA reappearance had 90.3% sensitivity, 97.9% specificity, 87.5% PPV and 98.4% NPV for confirmed recurrence, and preceded clinical recurrence in 58.1% of cases. Positivity one month after treatment was strongly associated with worse OS (HR 19.1) and DFS (HR 15.4), whereas the immediate post-treatment result carried little prognostic value. Single-institution, retrospective analysis of prospectively collected tests.
   **Why it matters:** A negative surveillance test identifies patients at very low short-term risk, which is the argument for leaner imaging follow-up; the one-month time point is the one to watch.
   [Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108126) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42715636/) · [U-M full text](https://proxy.lib.umich.edu/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1016/j.oraloncology.2026.108126)

8. **[URONCOR 06-24 interim: moderately hypofractionated salvage RT after prostatectomy has early toxicity similar to conventional](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctro.2026.101276)**
   _Clin Transl Radiat Oncol · Aug 30, 2026 · Genitourinary_ · phase 3 · hypofractionation · salvage RT · toxicity
   Prespecified interim analysis of the first 250 patients in this multicentre phase 3 trial; 175 received moderate hypofractionation (mostly 62.5 Gy/25) and 75 conventional fractionation (66–70 Gy/33–35). At 12.7 months, grade ≥2 GU events were 21.7% vs 17.3% (p = 0.43) and grade ≥2 GI 10.8% vs 4.0% (p = 0.079), with no clinically meaningful PRO differences; pelvic nodal RT was more common in the hypofractionated group (39.4% vs 13.3%) and was the factor linked to GI toxicity. Fractionation was not the randomised variable, so selection bias is possible.
   **Why it matters:** Reassuring early safety for 25-fraction salvage RT, but wait for the randomised comparison and late toxicity before calling it equivalent.
   [Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctro.2026.101276) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42741462/) · [U-M full text](https://proxy.lib.umich.edu/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ctro.2026.101276)

9. **[Randomised trial: compressing HDR brachytherapy into one week shortens overall treatment time and raises complete response in cervical…](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brachy.2026.07.163)**
   _Brachytherapy · Sep 10, 2026 · Gynecologic_ · randomised · brachytherapy · cervix · treatment time
   75 women with stage IB2–IVA cervical cancer on definitive chemoradiation were randomised to complete HDR brachytherapy within one week (n = 41) or over the conventional three weeks (n = 34); 68 were analysed at 5 years. Median overall treatment time fell from 64.5 to 59 days (p = 0.01) and complete response rose from 31.3% to 53.1% (p = 0.041), with no significant difference in OS (p = 0.96) or DFS (p = 0.13) and comparable acute and late proctitis, cystitis and bleeding. Small, single-country trial run in 2016–2017.
   **Why it matters:** Another nudge that the brachytherapy calendar, not just the dose, is a modifiable variable.
   [Article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brachy.2026.07.163) · [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42722513/) · [U-M full text](https://proxy.lib.umich.edu/login?url=https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brachy.2026.07.163)

10. **[REMATCH: response-adapted, reduced-volume surgery after neoadjuvant immunochemotherapy in HPV-negative HNSCC](https://doi.org/10.1158/1078-0432.ccr-26-1337)**
   _Clin Cancer Res · Sep 10, 2026 · Head & neck_ · phase 2 · de-escalation · neoadjuvant IO · larynx preservation

## Radiation oncology — practice, technique & physics

- **[Even palliative RT causes lymphopenia, and 30 Gy/10 triples the odds compared with a single 8 Gy fraction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijrobp.2026.08.072)** — _Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys, Sep 12_
  Retrospective institutional series of 2,002 palliative RT courses over 12 years; of 1,125 patients with normal pre-treatment counts, 748 developed lymphopenia. Median absolute lymphocyte count fell by 0.27, 0.49 and 0.66 for 8 Gy/1, 20 Gy/5 and 30 Gy/10 respectively (p < 0.01); on multivariable analysis 5 fractions (OR 1.85) and 10 fractions (OR 3.12) were more likely than one fraction to induce lymphopenia, and treatment site did not matter. One more reason to favour single-fraction schedules in patients on, or heading to, immunotherapy.

  Retrospective series of 155 patients (2022–2025) treated with a template-based simulate-plan-treat workflow on an integrated CT-linac. - **[Systematic review: aim for testosterone <20 ng/dL on ADT with RT; recovery after ADT is slow, variable and often incomplete](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.radonc.2026.111777)** — _Radiother Oncol, Sep 12_
  Twenty-nine studies addressing testosterone monitoring and replacement in intermediate/high-risk prostate cancer treated with 6–36 months of ADT plus curative RT (literature search run with an AI research agent under investigator supervision). Baseline testosterone did not predict oncologic outcome but did predict recovery; on-treatment suppression below 20 ng/dL rather than 50 ng/dL was associated with better oncologic outcomes; non-castrate levels returned within 6–15 months but full eugonadal recovery took years and was incomplete for many, slower with older age, low baseline testosterone and GnRH agonists.
